# Story for short-form edits

Practitioner rules (vendor guides and creator data, not controlled studies),
cross-checked against the vidIQ outliers we pulled in October 2026.

## Shape
1. **Hook, 0–3 s.** Lead with the strongest frame or claim. Promise →
   proof → payoff compressed: show the stakes before explaining them.
   Black-and-white slow motion plus one bold line is a proven football hook.
2. **Open loop.** Pose a question the viewer needs answered ("they called
   him... the villain"). Do not answer it in the first half.
3. **Escalate.** Each beat adds one idea and raises intensity: quieter
   shots first, bigger moments later. Change something visual every 2–4 s
   (cut, zoom, angle, graphic), but hold emotional shots 1–2 s longer.
4. **Turn on the drop.** Put the meaning flip where the music hits.
5. **Payoff in the final third.** An image that answers the hook: not just
   text. Reward viewers who stayed without giving it away early.
6. **Loop or CTA.** End on a frame that flows into the opening, or a
   question / tag prompt ("villain or GOAT?", "tag the one who...").

## Formats that were outliers (Oct 2026 sample)
- Beat-synced athlete montage, static caption on top (most common).
- Moody relatable caption over slow footage: "I care, I just don't show
  it anymore" (36x), "born to love football, not to become a footballer"
  (25x).
- Faceless text tribute over one strong image (808x): "I didn't grow up
  watching football. I grew up watching Messi."
- Legacy/rivalry stories: number 10 → number 19 handover.

## Writing on-screen text
- One idea per card, 2–6 words, lowercase for relatable, caps for hype.
- Pair every line with a picture that shows it. If the line says "no
  crowd", the shot is an empty stand, not the same pitch again.
- Use real facts for stats (check them); never invent numbers.
- Key word highlighted in one accent colour per edit.

## Emotion checklist
- Is there a person (or a person's trace: boots, shadow, breath) in most
  shots? Edits without people feel like screensavers.
- Does the music carry the emotion on its own? If muted music would make
  the edit stronger, the track is wrong.
- Would the viewer feel something at the turn, or just see an effect?
