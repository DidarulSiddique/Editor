The most famous Egyptian queen in history... was not Egyptian. At least, not the way you think.
You see, her family was Greek. They had ruled Egypt for two hundred and fifty years, and no king before her is known to have learned Egyptian.
Imagine you're a farmer on the Nile. You walk into the palace to beg for help... and your pharaoh needs a translator just to understand you.
How is that possible?
It starts with a stolen body. When Alexander the Great dies, his general Ptolemy hijacks the funeral cart... and drives Alexander's corpse all the way to Egypt. With the most famous corpse in the world as his trophy, he makes himself king.
His family rules from Alexandria, a Greek city. They speak Greek, they marry their own siblings, and they never bother with Egyptian.
It's like a landlord who owns your building for two hundred and fifty years... and never learns your name.
Then Cleopatra the Seventh is born, and she does the homework. She learns Egyptian. She dresses as a pharaoh. She calls herself the new Isis. And she talks to her people without a translator.
Egypt never forgot it. Four hundred years after she died, a scribe at an Egyptian temple was still covering her image... in gold.
