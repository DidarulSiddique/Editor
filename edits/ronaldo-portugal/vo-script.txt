The Ronaldo who won Portugal's first ever trophy... was not the same player who started. Twenty-three years. Four different players. One shirt.

Twenty-oh-three. An eighteen-year-old with frosted tips makes his debut. Number seventeen. Stepovers, flicks... and not much end product. Euro two-thousand-four, at home. Portugal lose the final to Greece, and he cries on the pitch.

Then the tricks get a purpose. By two-thousand-eight he's captain, and a winger who scores. Free kicks, headers, shots from thirty yards.

Next, less flash, more output. He stops beating men for fun, and starts cutting inside to finish. Sweden, the World Cup play-off: a hat-trick to send Portugal through. Euro twenty-sixteen: three goals to reach the final. Then he's injured after twenty-five minutes... and coaches from the touchline as Éder wins it. Portugal's first major trophy.

Then the legs change, so the game changes. Fewer touches. More movement. He lives in the box. A hat-trick in the Nations League semi-final, and Portugal win it. The all-time international scoring record. And at forty, the equaliser in another Nations League final.

Three trophies. A hundred and forty-six goals. And the first man to score at six World Cups.
